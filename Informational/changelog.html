Week 2 · Jul 27 – Aug 2, 2026

The forge learns to make heroes with more than one calling. Multiclassing arrives whole: a class ledger with prerequisite-gated cards, spell slots that pool across classes by the real rules while pact magic stands apart, hit dice that remember which class rolled them, and a character sheet that reads "Fighter 5 / Wizard 3" like it always could. Heroes saved before any of this existed load untouched. The starting kit stops speaking in categories, too: where the book says "any martial weapon", you now say which one.

Friday, July 31 — day two

0b1ef21POLISH The shield you chose is the shield you carry

Picking “martial weapon and shield” used to be a statement about your weapon only — the shield went unmentioned, the Armor Worn box stayed unticked, and your AC was two short until you noticed. Choosing an option that comes with a shield now ticks the box for you, and giving that option up drops the shield again unless something else in the kit still hands you one. The cleric, whose shield is not a choice at all, starts with it ticked the moment the class is chosen, and the box says where it came from. A shield you ticked yourself is never taken away behind your back.
898a145FEATURE “Any martial weapon” finally lets you say which one

Half the starting kits in the book hand you a category rather than an item — any martial weapon, two simple melee weapons, an arcane focus, a musical instrument, a set of artisan's tools — and the forge had no way to answer. It picked a longsword on your behalf, labelled it an example, and left it there; a fighter who wanted a greatsword simply could not have one. Every such slot now comes with its own dropdown, listing exactly the weapons that qualify with their damage dice on the label, and two-weapon options get two dropdowns so you can take a glaive in one hand's worth of choice and a longsword in the other. What you pick is written back into the kit list, the character sheet and the attack table, so the greatsword rolls its own 2d6 rather than the longsword's d8. Anything still unchosen is called out in the step and in the sheet's list of unfinished business, and changing the option a slot came from forgets the pick that no longer applies.
c33d95dFIX Dice answer to a finger, not just a mouse

Dragging a rolled die onto an ability worked beautifully on desktop and did nothing at all on a phone — the drag machinery browsers give you only listens to mice, and a touch never fires any of it. A finger drag now lifts a ghost of the die that follows the touch, lights up the ability box under it, and drops with exactly the same assign-and-swap rules as the mouse. The dice also claim their own touch gesture, so the page no longer scrolls out from under a drag.
—TOOLING This devlog

The page you are reading, plus the stats panel above it. One self-contained file in the generator's own colors — the numbers are written into it rather than fetched, and the d20 is CSS rather than a picture, so it opens from anywhere. The stats come out of the git history and the source itself, and any of them that cannot be worked out simply disappears rather than showing you a zero. From here on, every change to the forge gets an entry.
e882017FEATURE One hero, many callings — multiclassing arrives

The Advancement step becomes a class ledger. Your starting class sits in a row with a level stepper, and beneath it an "Add a class" drawer offers the other twenty-one — each card showing its ability-score prerequisite, and each locked card saying exactly why you don't qualify, so the rule teaches itself. The real rule is enforced in both directions: you need 13s for the class you are entering and for every class you already have. A new class grants only its reduced multiclass proficiencies — never full saves or a second pile of starting equipment — and Bard, Ranger and Rogue dips hand over their one skill pick. Every class keeps its own subclass, chosen at its own level, and its own ability improvements and feats at its own milestones. Character level is the sum, capped at twenty, and proficiency follows it.
e882017FEATURE Spell slots that pool; pact magic that doesn't

A lone caster keeps its own slot table, quirks and all. The moment a second slot-caster joins, the slots pool at a combined caster level — full casters count every level, half-casters half (the Artificer rounds up, as is its way), third-casters a third — read off the shared full-caster table. But each class still learns spells as if it stood alone: a Wizard 3 / Cleric 3 has the slots of a sixth-level caster and the spell lists of two third-level ones, which is exactly the trap and the treat the rule intends. Warlock pact magic joins none of this pooling; it stacks its own short-rest slots on top.
e882017POLISH Old heroes walk into the new rules unharmed

Every character saved before multiclassing existed loads exactly as it was — a single-class hero is simply a ledger with one row. Hit points now remember which class each level came from, rolling the right die for it, and the sheet shows mixed hit dice like 5d10+3d6. The identity line reads the whole story — "Level 8 Human Fighter 5 / Wizard 3 (School of Evocation)" — and the completeness warnings name the class they are nagging you about.

Week 1 · Jul 26, 2026

The whole forge is raised in a single Sunday: one HTML file that rolls dice, walks eight steps from ability scores to a printable parchment sheet, and knows the SRD by heart — nine races and twelve classes at first, then ten more callings and the full climb from level 1 to 20 with subclasses, feats and spell slots. By nightfall it has a print stylesheet, a license, and its trademarks filed off.

Sunday, July 26 — day one

ab960c1TOOLING Licensed, attributed, unbranded

MIT license for the code, proper Creative Commons attribution for the SRD material it draws on, and every trademarked name filed off in favor of the open-content equivalents. The generator stands on the System Reference Documents and says so plainly at the foot of every printed sheet.
5b6c10aPOLISH The sheet prints like a sheet

A real print stylesheet: the dark forge chrome falls away and the character sheet comes out as clean parchment pages, with the nav, buttons and dice tray left behind on the screen where they belong.
789d17cPOLISH Attacks move in under Saving Throws

The sheet's attack table stacks beneath the saving throws so the Features column can breathe. A high-level hero's feature list is the longest thing on the page; it gets the width.
0ed2d75POLISH Build choices live in Advancement, and drawers stay open

Skills and spells are chosen where levels are chosen, instead of being scattered across steps. And the page remembers which drill-down drawers you had open: the app re-renders wholesale on every change, and before this, each click slammed shut the very section you were working in.
002f832FIX Subclass cards answer when clicked

The archetype cards looked clickable and were not — the click listener's list of things it cared about simply didn't include them. A Champion could be admired but never chosen.
238ae38FEATURE Levels 1–20, and ten more callings

The generator stops being a first-level toy. A full advancement engine arrives: experience thresholds, subclasses unlocking at their proper levels, ability score improvements and feats, hit points averaged or rolled, and spell slots that know a full caster from a half, a third, and a warlock. Ten classes beyond the SRD twelve join the roster — Artificer to Pugilist — with progressions, subclasses and spell lists of their own, and the spell compendium grows to cover the levels the slots can now reach.
918547cFEATURE A face, a body, and dice you can drag

Rolled ability scores become draggable dice you drop onto abilities — and drop onto an occupied one to swap. The hero gets a body: height, weight, hair, eyes and skin drawn from race-appropriate tables, a portrait you can paste into the sheet, and an attacks table that knows finesse from heavy and proficiency from wishful thinking.
3c3d59bFEATURE The forge lights

Forge of Heroes exists: a fifth-edition-compatible character generator in one HTML file, no build step, no server, nothing to install. Eight steps walk a hero from rolled abilities through race, class, background, equipment and persona to a finished character sheet, with a dice tray in the corner and every choice saved to the browser as it is made. Open the file, roll, play.
